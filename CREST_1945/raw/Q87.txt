NORTH KOREA TO ENFORCE COMPULSORY EDUCATION; 
STUDENTS DENOUNCE COMMUNIST TEACHINGS 


COMPULSORY EDUCATION FOR AGES 7-15 -- Wen-hui Pao, 28 Jan 50 
Pyongyang, 27 January (Hsin-hua-she) -- The cabinet meeting of the People's 


Republic, held 11 January, formulated concrete plans for establishment of com- 
pulsory education. It is to be enforced throughout the Democratic People's Re- 


People's Assembly. 


Compulsory education will start from 1 September 1950. On that date all 
children, age 7 to 15, residing in the northern half of the People's Republic 
are required to enroll in people's schools to receive primary education. All 
parents and guerdiens of school-age children are required by law to have in 
their possession a certificate showing that the children under their guardian- 
ship are receiving, or have received, the compulsory education. Parents and 
guardians who do not obtain a certificate will be punished. 


All pupils are entitled to free education. However, the children of out- 
standing Communiets, members of the People's Army, Defense Corps, Peace-Preser- 
vation Corps, ard of disabled workers will receive free text books and writing 
paper from the government. Also, children of families sending five or more 
to the people's schools Simultaneously will get the same benefits. 


A general survey of all school-age children in North Korea is now under 
way. According to the data announced previously by the Supreme People's As- 
sembly, there were 1,320,000 children att2nding schools in North Korea as of 
1 September 1949. This number represented 94 percent of the total number of 
schcol-aze children in North Korea at that time. In P'yongyang, a 100-percent
