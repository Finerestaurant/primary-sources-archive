le On 1 September 300 men from a North Korean guerrilla unit stationed a 
Kosanemyon were split into three companies, with each company station 
on three different heights overlooking Samnang=jin (128-50, 35~23). 
Each man had a Japanese type 99 rifle, 100 rounds of ammunition; each 
15<man squad was equipped with subemachine guns. The unit was armed 
with three 60-mm, mortars, and two heavy machine guns. Their objecti ek 
were the observation of roads into the city, the sabotage of military 
installations, and the ambushing of UN and ROK troops, The unit is 9 
equipped with a radio, and reports regularly to the North Korean forces. 
The unit left the North Korean east coast on 23 June and in late July 
arrived at Kosan-myon, 12 km. east of Samnang-jin. 


2. On the evening of 30 August 5,000 NKPA troops gathered from the moun- 
tains around Ipam-ni and marched towards Angangeni (129-14, 35-59). 
The troops were armed with Soviet machine guns, rifles, U.S. carbines, 
Japanese Model 38 or 99 rifles, a small munber of mountain guns and 
60 mortars. Each soldier was equipped with five hand grenades; only 
one third of the troops had any other weapon, Unarmed soldiers were 
told to use the weapons of fallen comrades or captured U.S. equipment, 
Sixty percent of these troops are South Koreans drafted for the Volunteer 
Army and given 2 weeks' training. 


3. There was a rumor among North Korean troops in the Chongsong (129-0), 36=26) 
area on 23 August that the NKPA canmander ordered units lacking supplies 
to withdraw to the Seoul area if all of the ROK was not occupied by the 
end of August. Units still receiving supplies were ordered to fight to 
the end and to continue to maintain the front. Also, late in August there 
was a rumor in the Chongsong area that NKPA troops on the east coast would 
be relieved by troops from the Inchon area.
