The People's Republic of North Korea presented the working people of North 
Korea with every opportunity for developing the trade-union movement. In Nov- 
ember 1945, conferences of the following branch trade unions were held: miners, 
railroad workers, chemists, metalworkers, workers in education, textile workers, 
construction workers, workers in the food industry, and workers in the fish and 
timber industries. A’ the end of November of the same year, the first congress 
of the trade unions of North Korea was held, attended by delegates from 853 trade 
union organizations, comprising 190,000 workers and office employees. The con- 
gress formed the Joint Committee of Workers of North Korea, composed of 14 branch 
trade unions. In May 1946, the Joint Committee was zenemed the North Korea Fed- 
eration of Trade Unions. 


With the land reform and the nationalization of transport, communications, 
banks, and of the most important branches of industry, more and more workers were 
drawn into the trade unions. In June 1946, there were 312,000 persons in the 
trade unions of North Korea, 467,000 by the end of December 1948, and more than 
half a million by the beginning of 1950.
