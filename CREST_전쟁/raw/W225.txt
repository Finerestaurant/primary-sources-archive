DISCUSSES NORTH KOREAN PUBLICATIONS 


LOW QUALITY PREVAILS -- Seoul Ibuk T'ongsin, Dec kg 


Numerous periodicals and newspapers mushroomed in North Korea after the 
liberation. Among those still in publication is one called Munhwa Chonson, 
published by the North Korean Federation of Literature and Art. Despite the 
fact that this periodical is of low literary value, its publisher and chief 
editor, Han Sol-ya, and the staff editors, Yi Ki-yong, Yi Ch'an, An Ham-kwang , 
and Ch'oe Ki-ch'on, claim that it represents the latest literary trends of 
North Korea. It was originally a monthly publication, but due to printing 
difficulties it was reduced to a bi-monthly and finally to a quarterly. 


The Russo-Korean Cultural Society publishes its organ bearing the or- 
ganization's name, Choso Munhwa- Yi Ki-yong publishes this magazine 45 4 
mouthpiece for Soviet propaganda. The same staff that edits the Muphwa 
Choason also edits Chosc_Munhwa. Yi Ch'an is the chief editor. Yi Ch'an, 
@ seif-styled poet, became famous overnight in North Korea when he wrote, 
"The Song Dedicated to General Kim Il-song." Choso Muphwa usually contains 
only translations of Soviet articles, indiscriminately selected. The fact 
that this magazine is subsidized by the Soviet government is beyond all. 
doubt. Yi Ki-yong reportedly received a private automobile from the Soviet 
Ambassador Shtikov as a reward for his onedience to the USSR. 


Noteworthy is the fact that none of the names of the top-flight 
writers who went from South Korea ever appear in these magazines. These 
so-called "South Korean writers in exile" include Yi T'ae-chun, Yi Won-cho, 
Kim Nam-ch'on, Im Hwa. Chi Ho-yon, and many others. Whether these writers 
voluntarily shun these magazines or are deprived of the privilege of 
writing ids not known. 


Each of the above magazines publishes between 5,000 and 10,000 copies 
for each issue. They are inferior to South Korean publications in their j 
content, print, and the paper used.

‘ eration of Literature and Art. It ia edited by An Ham-kwang, a self-styled 
materialist and critic, who is also a member of the Central Standing Com-

The Central People's Committee of North Korea publishes its propaganda 
mouthpiece called, Inmin,which allegedly carries articles on "current affairs,"- 
but in fact is reserved for the full texts of speeches of Kim Il-song and his 
henchmen. The Jnmin 1s edited by T'ae Song-chu, an obscure individual. 


The Ministry of Education publishes Inmin Kyoyuk, which usually devotes 
ite pages. to ‘the translations of articles by Soviet educators. All schools 
are required to subscribe to this magazine. * 


There is a children's magazine, called Adong Munhak, which is perhaps the 
only magazine of sustained literary quality among the North Korean publications. 


The Kim Il-song University publishes a quarterly magazine called Yoksa. 
It contains translations of Soviet scientific articles. 


The T'aep'ung is the North Korean counterpart of Ibuk T'ongsin, in that 
both magazines expose the “inside stories" of South and North Korea, respece 
tively, and attack the government of the other. 


The so-called National People's Publishing Company puts out Sae Chosun 
which is perhaps the largest North Korean magazine in terms of size (usually 
190-page 8vo.) This magazine publishes industrial news, usually lengthy re- 
ports of staff members' vists to North Korean factories. Recent issues have 
also carried economic reports on South Korea and stories of guerrilla activi- 
tles on Cheju Island. A substantial part of this magazine is also reserved 
for Soviet propaganda. * th 


The Literary Front Society, located at 17 Ch'ang-chon-ri, P'yongyang, 
publishes Munhak Aesul. Only eight issues of it have been published so far. 


There is a Soviet Korean-lenguage publication called Kukche P'yongnon, 
which specializes in discussions of international affairs and is the counter- 


. part of the monthly periodical, “America in South Korea. Its publication is 
under the direct supervision of the Soviet Embassy. . 


The foremost scientific magazine of North Korea is the Chayon Kwahak, 
published by Peng Hui-yong in close cooperation with the professors of sci- 
ences of the Kim Il-song University.
